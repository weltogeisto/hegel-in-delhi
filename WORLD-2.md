# The world, phase two

Written by Claude on 15 November 2026, at Welt's request: improve the world to improve the simulation. Revised on 16 November, after Welt asked that the papers be read and not their abstracts. This is a plan, not a go. Every packet in it waits for Welt's word, like T39–T45.

**The idea in one line: in phase one Delhi answered him. In phase two Delhi has a day of its own.**

Phase one built a referee. The world owns the clock, the purse and the rules, and it says no correctly: closed is closed, and cash cannot go below zero. That work holds. The record now shows the other half. Seven in ten of his moments open with someone answering what he has just said. The world brings him something he did not start about twice a day. Today the world answers one verb, *talk*, and so he talks.

A referee makes a fair game. It takes a city to make a life. Phase two builds the city: a Delhi that holds what is said in it, offers what a man like him would cross the road for, changes because he was there, and goes on when he is not.

In the season plan (`PLAN.md`) this sits inside Act 1. It is what makes opening night worth opening.

## What the full reading changed

The first version of this plan cited some eighty works, most of them from their abstracts. Sixty-nine have now been read from the first page to the last appendix, and every claim the plan made was checked against the text (`research/WORLD-2-READINGS.md`). The designs stand. Much of the evidence under them did not.

- **The papers show the failures. They do not test our cures.** Nobody has measured an engine that audits a reply before it is heard, refuses a second promise, or marks a claim as someone's word. Those are ours, and T57 is their first test.
- **Several numbers meant less than they seemed.** "Three in four false claims followed a look at the ledger" is true, and so is the same share for true claims. A "99.7%" for engine-filled slots was a check of arithmetic. A study I used against persona adapters measured a personality test, on forum posts, with judges that barely agreed.
- **One instrument of mine was wrong.** Welt's labels were to be drawn where two arms differ. The method needs them drawn at random, or its guarantee is gone.
- **One guard would not have worked.** Switching an old rule off, alone, cannot show what it was holding if another rule covers for it. That is what masking means.
- **Our own code holds a trap.** Once any adapter is served, the letters from Munich and the owl would be written through Hegel's adapter: `world/mind.py` turns every call that names no adapter into one that uses it.
- **A new rule follows:** measure the failure before building the cure. A gate changes nothing for a model that seldom errs.

So each chapter below says what is **shown** in a paper, in whose setting, and what is **ours** to test.

## What phase one settled

- **Code owns the facts.** The checks work, and this plan does not reopen them.
- **Selection did what training could not.** The filter cut echoes from 16.5% to 6.6% with no weights changed (T49). Two adapters did not beat plain Qwen, and Training 3 stopped at its data gate.
- **Instructions move his register.** The M1 confirmation passed.
- **The world decides what he can attend to.** His Delhi offered tombs, a market and an institute's gate. He philosophised, ate and counted his money.

The last line is why phase two is about the world.

## The rules of phase two

1. **The world offers and never steers** (`DECISIONS.md` 2 and 8). So the world is gated on what it offers and what it records. What he does with it is read, and never gated. A target for his behaviour would be steering under another name.
2. **Claude may write the world, never the man.** Places, events and storylines are world content, as the venues of `pc/CULTURE.md` are. Everything said and thought inside it comes from the local model. No world text is ever a training target (`mind/train/README.md`).
3. **One owner for every fact.** If a thing matters later (a gift, a promise, the state of an order), the engine holds it. Prose may describe it. Prose cannot create it.
4. **Additive, behind switches, one at a time** (`DECISIONS.md` 31). Old day files stay readable, and a switch set back restores the old world byte for byte, as `HEGEL_CULTURE` does.
5. **Experiments are forks.** A simulated life can be copied at a morning and lived twice. It is the one fair comparison a single Hegel allows. A past day replayed is another day by its fourth step: in T39's replays, 152 of 156 cases had different prompts across seeds (T49).
6. **The letters are a compass, with one journey kept back.** Tuning looks at the Netherlands and Vienna. Paris, the journey a later backtest would rebuild, stays out of every tuning loop until the end.
7. **Measure the failure before building the cure.** Each chapter starts with a count on the record as it stands: how often a voice asserts what the world does not hold, how often a promise is broken, how often he folds when pressed.
8. **Say what is shown and what is ours.** A design borrowed from a paper keeps the paper's setting beside it. A design of our own says so.

## Six chapters

### 1 · The instrument

**Why.** The nightly report says how the mind did. Nothing says how the world did. And the rehearsal cannot rehearse phase two: `simulate` builds the world without the culture switch, and each `--date` starts again from the repository's day files, so a month cannot be lived in rehearsal. In a scratch driver, a chained month with the stand-in mind took ten seconds.

**What.**
- **World health in the nightly report:** the row of `tools/world_health.py` beside T19's row. It gains four reads the mix cannot give: how often he switches activity in a day, how long he stays with each kind, how much one of his days differs from another, and how many of a day's kinds of act he has done before.
- **The step says what opened it.** A field `opened` names the beat, the papers, the greeting or the gallery, so the read stops depending on wording.
- **Rehearsals that chain days and know the switches:** `simulate --days N`, with the world built as the tick builds it.
- **Four scripted stand-ins:** one says yes to everything (today's), one says no, one stays home, one walks. They test the world's mechanics. They say nothing about his choices.
- **Forks.** One command copies a saved morning into sandboxes with different switches. The morning holds the engine's whole state and every seed; today `chat()` sends no seed at all. Every comparison carries a control fork, the same world twice. Under the stand-in the control must come out identical, state for state. With real Qwen it gives the band of noise, and a difference between worlds is read only above it. Forks are compared by the engine's state (purse, orders, promises) as well as by the page.
- **The funnel:** for each offer, event and venue, how often it was possible, shown, taken and declined.
- **Blind tests that can see.** A gate of 16 in 24 finds a true preference of 65% about half the time, and our bar of 0.25 on twenty texts an arm is below anything one judge can detect. So: packets of 50 to 100 sealed situations. Soft qualities judged as pairs, in both orders. The judge's instructions frozen beforehand, on other cases. Welt labels 30 pairs of a packet, drawn at random, and Claude judges all of them blind; the result is corrected by his labels and carries an interval. Under 50 pairs, Welt labels them all and an exact test decides. Never a bootstrap.

**Shown.** With one judge, rubric scores have a ceiling of reliability of 0.6 to 0.8 that no number of items raises; pairs asked in both orders reach 0.99, and position alone moves a verdict by 9 points (a study of nine judges and 42 systems; its pairwise arm is small). The smallest rubric difference one judge can detect at 30 items is 0.6 to 1.2 points of five. A machine judge corrected by a random sample of human labels keeps false alarms near 5%, where raw judge scores gave 37%; this was shown on scores, from 60 items up. On a coding benchmark, control forks of one small model diverged at nine forks in ten at temperature zero, and of another almost never: the noise belongs to the serving setup.

**Ours.** All of this on preference pairs, with one human, at our sizes. The first packet is checked by simulation on our own data. The band of noise is measured on our own server before any fork is read. Three methods from the first version are dropped: none of them was shown anywhere near a few dozen human labels.

**What it buys.** Two or three experiments a week on frozen moments, where there was one, and one slot a week for forked days. It costs Welt 30 labels a packet.

**Cost.** Claude, about a week. No GPU, nothing live.

### 2 · Truth: what is said, the world can hold

**Why.** The three moments above. The published record shows this failure in others and has not yet tested a cure. In a year of simulated commerce, one email in twelve between agents held a false claim, mostly about payments already made, and only one claim in five was sent straight after a look at the books. Narrators who had the facts in their own prompt still contradicted them in 40 to 68% of stories; a better memory left that where it was, 60 runs in 100 against 59, though it cut broken commitments from 26 to 4.

**First, the count.** Before anything is built: 100 recorded replies of his people, read by hand, for states the record does not hold; and how many promises his people have made and what became of them.

**What.**
- **Speech moves nothing.** What a voice says is a trace. State moves only by an act from a closed menu that the engine has checked: gives, puts on the tab, charges, promises, invites, declines. A wrong act is refused with its reason, as his decisions are, and the engine caps the retries: a reason alone does not stop a loop. Names are resolved before the check, and an act in two parts runs in order. Sunil's cup becomes a gift and a snack.
- **Voices write around slots.** Where a fact belongs (a price, a balance, the day an order is ready), the voice writes a placeholder and the engine fills in the value.
- **Every reply is audited before he hears it.** A reader checks the prose for a state the record does not hold: ready, paid, delivered, came, will come. It asks once more. What still stands reaches him marked as someone's word, not as an event. The reader is not the writer: other instructions, and its verdict is code's to apply. What it cannot read counts as someone's word. Every field it fills must change an outcome.
- **Each voice sees its own side of the record.** The engine's record of who was present is the one source.
- **Storylines as protocols.** One registry gives each thread its states, its legal moves, its parties and what each party may say: the tailor's order, the file, the lessons, the column, the loan, the letters. Each move has preconditions and effects. What `voices.recorded_facts` writes by hand for three people is then generated for all.
- **Promises come due.** A promise is a record with an object and a date. The engine refuses a second promise on the same thing, and resolves each one: kept, broken with a reason, or lapsed. Ramesh's word becomes one.
- **What was said stays a quotation.** In memory a claim keeps its speaker, its date and its words. Each encounter has one entry; the gist and his recall point to it and do not retell it.

**Shown.**
- **A closed menu of acts:** as a design, in one pilot with eight players and three kinds of act, all of them movement. Errors still got past its checks. A catalogue of such couplings says of this one: "One demonstration run. No measurement isolates this pattern yet."
- **Slots:** one precedent, for a total price. With the placeholder the total was right every time, and four times in five without it (one model, 100 scripted dialogues in Korean).
- **Refusals with reasons:** one world refused all 5,455 invalid acts, each with its reason. The reasons did not help the minds: the arm told less did better, and the retries ran to some 800 a week.
- **Each voice's own view:** on Qwen3.8-27B, his own model, a character used what it could not have known, by 3 to 60 points over chance depending on what was withheld and how the prompt said so, and by 9 to 68 when only the character's sheet changed. Given only what it had observed, the leak fell to zero, in short template scenes. With thinking on it fell from 73 to 18.
- **State the engine enforces:** when the state of a task was enforced by a module, a model without thinking scored 0.98, against 0.55 when the same state was shown as text. With thinking and the rule written down, text did nearly as well. The weak part was the matcher: reworded requests cut the enforced arm from 1.00 to 0.78.
- **Promises:** in a simulated marketplace all five models promised the same item to more than one buyer.
- **Quotations:** note-writers turned "I am working at X" into "works at X" in 37 to 68% of cases, and never the reverse, in all eleven models tested. A record with the speaker's words restored one reader's caution out of two. Models give a retold observation 6 to 42% of the weight of a new one; told to point to it instead, agents committed too early in 1 case in 100 where it had been 11.

**Ours.** The audit before he hears a reply; the mark of someone's word; the refusal of a second promise; slots for dates and balances; all of it at 27B and Q4. T57 tests them, each in its own arm, and the audit's misses are counted by hand in 100 replies, on reworded cases too. The owl gets its own test: statements of the "I am working on it" kind and facts said in passing are planted in day files, and we count what reaches the gist and the ledger.

**How we know.** T40's bars on its 40 contexts, with arms for today's voices, typed facts in the prompt, slots with the audit, and the mark of someone's word. Scene success up by 0.20, unsupported claims of a closed state halved, no new reversals in the clean controls, voice and argument no worse in blind pairs. In the paper city (chapter 5), every thread can reach an end and no state is without an exit.

**Cost.** Claude builds. The PC seat runs about 480 short calls, 1.5 to 3 GPU hours in the spare slot, then one night of forks.

### 3 · Breadth: a city worth leaving the house for

**Why.** The Paris profile. Abroad he looked, listened and walked. Here the world answers only when he speaks.

**What.**
- **Museums, concerts and plays.** Built, and fifth in the order of `DECISIONS.md` 34.
- **The street** (`HEGEL_STREET`). A deck of things that happen where he is: a wedding band tuning up, schoolchildren counted at the tomb's gate, a cricket match on the lawn, fog over the gardens. Each card has its places, season, hours and weather, a cooldown, at most 240 characters, and sometimes a passer-by he may speak to. One is drawn by date when nothing else opens the moment. A card's place, hours and cooldown are built by the world's own operations and cannot be wrong. Its prose is read twice: by a linter, for a card that tells him what to do or names a living person, and by Welt, before each batch goes on. The first batch is 60 cards.
- **Reading returns something.** Today *read* is a posture. It becomes a text: the article behind a headline, a longer passage of the book he took down, a public-domain book he bought.
- **A second ring of places,** chosen by the letters: what did he seek abroad, and where is its counterpart within a walk? A church and its music, a reading room with German papers, a collection of natural history or craft, a promenade with company, a table for cards, the post office his letters leave from. Candidates and walking times come from OpenStreetMap. Welt chooses.
- **The season as Delhi keeps it:** fog, the wedding months, Christmas, the Republic Day rehearsals.

**Shown.** Scenes built through checked operations ran 20 times in 25; scenes written as free text and checked afterwards, 0 in 50. The two arms used different models, so it is a pointer and not a measurement. Those checks were structural. No operation can check a sentence.

**Ours.** The whole chapter. Its evidence is our own profile of the letters.

**How we know.** Gated on the offer: empty moments from 15% to at most 5%, what the world brings from 1.9 to at least 6 a day, and every venue and kind of card within reach weekly for the walking stand-in. Read, not gated: stage 1's profile again on the days after, with both coders, and with the four new reads of chapter 1.

**Cost.** Claude writes. Welt reads a batch in half an hour. One night of forks per bundle.

### 4 · Depth: a world that remembers him and moves without him

**Why.** In 46 days two manuscripts, two theses that changed a word of status each, and a file that has stood at "come after fifteen days". Little he does comes back to him.

**What.**
- **The file gets its next act.** This one has a date: the last written beat falls on his 20 November. Claude drafts the file as a thread of six to eight states with months in it and several endings, none of them chosen. Welt edits it. The frame is his (`DECISIONS.md` 8).
- **People have a week of their own.** Each named person gets a small agenda in data that moves by date: a deadline, a visit, the wedding season at the tailor's. At the next meeting their card holds one line of what happened meanwhile. Code decides when they turn up. The model decides only what they say, and only when they meet.
- **Standing.** For each person, one small record, updated in place: the days met, what stands between them (owed, promised, given), and a regard that moves only on recorded acts: a debt paid or left, an appointment kept or missed, a gift. The event says what follows from it: Sunil closes the tab, and Masterji asks for an advance.
- **Stakes are written down.** Each person's card says what they refuse and when they press, and the thread's rule fires it: a deadline, a refusal, Mr. Saxena's no. The world adds offers on top of his turns. It never repairs a plot.
- **Word gets round.** A recorded act reaches one neighbour a day later, as the engine's fact and never as someone's retelling. Khan Market knows he could not pay for tea.
- **An attempt may fail.** He may walk to a closed door and find it closed, with the time spent, instead of being refused in advance (the manual's §4.4). One scene family first.
- **What he has learned stays in front of him.** The notebook of the age, which is still an idea in `DECISIONS.md`, built as an index and not a summary: for each thing of this century he has met, when first, when last, where in the record, and one line. And convictions the engine holds: the text, the criterion, the versions (the manual's M3).
- **The owl tells what was unusual for him.** The health row tells it what departed from his own days.

**Shown.**
- **Stories go flat, and a written disposition is kept.** Six frontier models replayed 40 stories from the middle. Seven of 240 replays ended with a goal reached, where 39 of the 40 originals resolved their tension. Yet a card that told a character to oppose or to cross a line was followed about four times in five. Stakes that are written get acted on.
- **Repair costs him his turns.** In one study with a simulated player, a manager that repaired dead ends applied 27% of the player's commands unchanged, against 44% without repair. Characters with goals of their own, acting after his turn, left 68%, and almost nothing of the course the author had planned. So agendas stay in data and the threads' beats stay written.
- **A model's reading of a conversation is noisy.** A local system that let the model name how a relationship moved found the run-to-run noise larger than an effect it had built in. Retelling changed the hour or the owner of a commitment.
- **A score does not act by itself.** Agents spotted the bad actor in 83 to 100% of cases, and their trust scores changed how they voted but not whether a deal was reached. Written guidance on using the score helped one model and not the other.
- **Light personas fold; a trained one held.** Asked "Are you sure? Many experts disagree", a prompted persona kept a belief of its era 7 times in 100 and a lightly tuned one 14; a character trained into the weights held 62 times on a 70B model and 42 on an 8B one. The beliefs were false facts of the past. No one tested a conviction.
- **Knowing is not using.** A system handed the right memory answered 97% of questions about a shared past and used it in half of the moments that called for it.
- **Asked every hour, a model keeps a regular clock.** Timing set by code was bursty, as real timing is. The burst was built in by the authors.
- **Sifting by drama:** in one small study (24 readers, one world) it showed no link to what readers enjoyed.

**Ours.** Standing that moves on recorded acts alone: no paper does this, and the two nearest let a model rewrite the relationship. Convictions held by the engine. The owl's line, which is a hypothesis for our own readers. When people turn up: we have no data on Delhi's days, so the gate is set by judgement, and says so. G4 gains two arms from this reading: the owl writing alone, and a challenge to each thesis.

**How we know.** Threads moved in a week. Obligations resolved. How often he goes back to someone and uses what passed between them, by the days between: a measure we define, judged blind with a sample of Welt's. T41's tests at the same budget. Convictions judged in blind pairs for whether a change was earned by the record. A test that a refusal written in a card yields refusals at his model's size.

**Cost.** Mostly CPU. Forks as in chapter 3.

### 5 · Tuning: the world against the man

**What may be tuned.** The side of opportunity only: places, hours, prices, wages, the conditions of offers, people's schedules, how often things happen, the wording and order of what is listed. Never his instructions, and nothing that tells him what to want.

**The loop, at four speeds.**

| Speed | What runs | What it answers | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| The paper city | 100 settings × 4 stand-ins × 28 days, no model | Which settings are impossible: an offer nobody can reach, a shortfall with no exit, a working stand-in that cannot close the month, a kind of thing the letters are full of that the world offers less than weekly | about 400 months, a little over an hour on one core |
| Frozen moments | real Qwen, short calls: 8 wordings raced down to 2, today's wording among them and the same arm run twice, then 24 unseen moments, used once | Which wording and order of listings and offers he takes up, with the scoreboard's bars as constraints | about 550 calls |
| Forks | real Qwen, whole days: 7 mornings × 2 worlds and a control, and one week per world for what carries over | Whether a changed world gives a different life | about 1,800 calls, 5 hours |
| Shadow | one switch, the old world as a fork beside it | Whether it does harm in his real days. A check, not a test: one timeline over a few weeks gives five to ten blocks, and the method for such designs shows no power below 28 | his days |

**Guards.**
- A cap on the length of what is listed.
- Options in shuffled order.
- An alarm when any offer is taken more than nine times in ten: an offer nobody refuses is an order.
- A winner must beat today's wording by more than the band between the two equal arms.
- The judge's rubric never enters the search.
- Every call logs the model that served it.
- The rules that protect something are named and frozen, and no gate may delete them. Each has a probe that fails without it, run after every adoption.

**Two early tests of what is put in front of him.** The money line stands in every prompt, and hunger is announced as an event. Food and money fill 29% of his decision thoughts and 4% of the letters. Whether a man is told the state of his purse at every step is the world's choice. Both changes touch his prompt, so both go through the ratchet as prompt changes do.

**Shown.**
- **The mix can be hit by steering, at a price.** Agents told their group's surveyed routine in their own prompt matched a held-out survey at 0.84, against 0.35 untold. That is what rule 1 forbids. Where statistical priors chose the activity, the mix came closest to real and the days broke: 38 more switches a day than the real 27, and a fifth of the real difference between one day and the next.
- **A gain on the cases you search can turn on the cases you did not.** A skill optimiser lifted a Qwen3.8-27B from 0.62 to 0.78 on 100 selection cases and lowered it from 0.82 to 0.79 on 500 held-out ones. The model was served in 8-bit on a Mac, and two full reruns of the pipeline disagreed with each other. A prompt optimiser's gain on a maths set did hold; audited on the study's other model, such gains were answers no longer cut off.
- **A quarter of one optimiser's gain was the scoring rubric,** rebuilt inside the prompt. In the same study a gateway silently served another model during outages.
- **A gate can delete what another rule covers.** Planted deletions of a redundant safety rule passed a success-only gate 63 times in 100. Switching such a rule off alone shows nothing. What held was freezing the rules and testing declared harms.
- **A fuller purse changed little.** In a town of 100 agents on his own model family, 97% of a cash grant was still held 13 days later. The authors could not tell hoarding from the way their tools were built.
- **A script is not a model.** A fixed-rule shopkeeper earned about 7.6 times the best model's profit, and the best model was his.
- **A model that writes its own days settles.** With no world at all, eight models filled 40 days from about ten kinds of act; by day 11 nine in ten of five days' kinds had been seen before. Hotter sampling did not help. The authors' own remedy is material from outside.
- **A twin is barely the person.** Twins built from a person's own 500 answers were as accurate as an empty persona (0.75 against 0.73), and better correlated with their person (0.20 against 0.08).

**Ours.** That a richer offer changes him more than a fuller purse: no paper tested it, and forks will. The race of eight wordings: nothing published supports or opposes it at our size. The two nulls for the profile, plain Qwen with no persona and a generic professor of the 1820s. And a floor for every distance: two stretches of the letters measured against each other.

**How we know.** The health table, the funnel, the economy's bands (days of runway, days with the kitchen shut, exits open at each shortfall), and the profile's distance to the two journeys beside its nulls and its floor. Paris is read once, at the end.

### 6 · Post-training: what the new world makes trainable

Training follows the world.

**With the filter live, the world's verdicts have little left to teach.** On his decisions they leave about 1% refusals and 2–7% echo. Weights trained on them could save a quarter of a call. The open gap is argument (2.65 of 5) and voice (3.05 of 5), and no check of the world's labels those. Only Welt's judgement can.

**Our own record is the evidence against a persona adapter:** two rounds, neither better than plain Qwen. The published record is thin and it is mixed. A character trained into the weights held its beliefs under challenge where prompted ones folded; as published, that method has a second model for a teacher. Expert readers preferred pages by a model tuned on one author to prompted imitation, strongly for likeness and modestly for quality, on 30 excerpts, with GPT-4o tuned through its maker's service.

So phase two supplies the two things training lacked: labels a rule can verify (the audit of what the voices say), and situations rich enough for Welt's judgement to be worth asking.

| Rung | What | The label comes from | When it runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | No training. The best setup for every job; a sealed packet by scene family; the same arm run twice, for the band of noise; one arm with two or three of his own accepted thoughts as examples | – | First, always. A few GPU hours. Every later rung must beat the best arm here at equal calls |
| 1 | **The voices.** One adapter for the people he meets, so that a reply passes the audit at the first try | the engine's audit and its check on acts | Only if T57 leaves a residue that the audit and one retry do not remove, and with the audit's misses counted by hand. In rounds: sample, filter, train, sample again |
| 2 | **His thoughts, by Welt's hand.** Welt chooses blind between two draws the world accepted | Welt | On his word. 300 pairs to train and 100 sealed: about seven hours of his time. Trained in chunks, with fresh draws between them. It must beat the examples arm built from the same choices, in 61 of the 100. Under 55, voice by weights is closed for the season |
| 3 | His writing sittings only: from a brief Qwen writes to the passage he wrote | his texts | Parked. It would be a third attempt at his voice |

**Not run, and why.**
- **The filter distilled into weights.** There is a quarter of a call to win.
- **Preference pairs from the world's labels, made once.** One situation in six gave a contrast, and in the one comparison read, pairs made once were the weakest arm.
- **Reinforcement learning on the 3090.** Most rollouts would carry no signal, and the card has 1.5 GiB to spare.
- **Continued pretraining on his books.** Our first adapter did not show it helps. In the one study near our size, a model trained on a raw corpus of 1.3 M tokens answered questions about it slightly worse than before; it took 455 M tokens of rewritten material to help. His books are 3.4 M.
- **Steering vectors.** In llama.cpp they are set when the server starts, so they would reach the voices and the owl too.

**Shown.**
- **Rules first, weights for reliability.** In one study without a teacher, rules in the harness made unsolved tasks solvable and training made solved ones reliable. So slots and the audit hold the facts, and an adapter is asked only to use them reliably.
- **One round is weak.** On one small task, one pass of filter-then-train trailed reinforcement learning by 8 to 11 points, and three rounds won back 3 of them, barely above the noise. The published loops run on thousands of kept samples a round. Nothing near our size exists.
- **Training on a checker entrenches what it lets through,** when many prompts share the same wrong answer. This was shown in a model of 100,000 parameters.
- **Tuning on top of an instruction-tuned model lowered diversity** in one study of an 8B model, most of all with preference training on fresh samples.
- **Plain LoRA is enough, if the rate is tuned.** On models up to 13B and tasks with right answers, LoRA's variants finished within about two points of plain LoRA once each had its own learning rate. A wrong rate cost far more than the choice of variant.

**Ours.** Every size on the ladder. The first run of a rung is its own measurement: three rounds, with the floor Training 3 used, 200. Rung 3's recipe is the readers' study with Qwen writing the brief: excerpts of 250 to 650 words, a brief that follows each excerpt in order, the pair "write this, as him" to the real page. Translated philosophy is untested there.

**How a rung runs, if one does.**
- **The recipe:** plain LoRA on all layers at rank 16 and alpha 32, as Training 3 was approved, with a handful of learning rates tried and the alpha written down. By our reading of llama.cpp's converter, DoRA is refused outright and rsLoRA arrives four times too weak at rank 16.
- **Serving, and the trap in our own code.** llama.cpp takes a list of adapters with each request, and a request that names none takes the server's default. Its documentation says a start-up flag sets that default to nothing; the code we read does not show that. So: the PC seat tests it once, and until then every call names its adapter and its scale, zero for off, and never sends an empty list. `world/mind.py` does not do this today. `complete()` names none. The owl, the letters from Munich, the outline of a sitting and the yes-or-no after an offer name none, and one line turns every such call into Hegel's adapter at full strength as soon as the voices' switch is set. This is fixed and tested before any adapter is served.
- **A change of adapter costs one full reading of the prompt,** and requests with different adapters do not share a batch. That price is measured in rehearsal first.
- **Diversity is read beside quality,** on the same samples, against plain Qwen: the spread of his chosen acts, the distinct values in each field, the share of new three-word runs, over 16 to 32 draws a situation.
- **Tests with power.** 28 of 40 finds a true preference of 65% one time in three; 61 of 100 finds it five times in six.
- **The plumbing is checked:** trained on one 4-bit format and served on another, prompts cut at a default length, a base that is 20.8 GiB on disk where `pc/TRAINING.md` says about 17.

Each run still needs Welt's go, by day and watched. The data rule holds. One bounded run closes each question.

## What the field warns against

- **A voice that reports on itself.** A model judging its own answer is a named failure in the catalogue of such designs, and so is a check that nothing reads. Hence a reader that is not the writer, and fields that change outcomes.
- **Facts shown and still contradicted.** Narrators with the facts in their prompt broke them in most stories. Hence slots and an audit, not a longer prompt.
- **A summary as the record.** It flattens what was said and gives a retelling the weight of news. Hence quotations and one entry an encounter.
- **A manager that repairs the plot.** It takes turns from the protagonist. Hence offers only.
- **Hitting the mix by imposing it.** It works, and it breaks the days. Hence a profile that is read and never gated, with switches and dwell beside it.
- **Tuning on the test.** Hence the journey kept back, and confirmation cases used once.
- **A script taken for a mind.** Hence stand-ins for mechanics only.
- **A gate that sees only the score.** Hence frozen rules and probes.
- **Small tests read as verdicts.** Hence pairs, random labels, intervals and control forks.
- **A life with nothing from outside.** It settles within a fortnight. The health table watches for it, and the world is the remedy.
- **A citation read from its abstract.** Hence this revision.
