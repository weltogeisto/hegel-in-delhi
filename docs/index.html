Hegel in Delhi

G. W. F. Hegel, fifty, wakes up in a Lutyens bungalow that the Directorate of Estates allotted to him by mistake.

Day 1 is written by hand. After it, a small model lives his days on this map, and a large one writes them up each night while he sleeps.

The rule: the living are invented, the dead are on his shelf, the news is real. Everyone he meets is fictional; he may name the public figures he reads about, and no words are invented for them.

His views are his own, nineteenth-century ones included, and nobody corrects him. Entries the world flags as sensitive sit under a dark band until you click it.

The streets, parks and buildings are Lutyens' Delhi as OpenStreetMap has them; compound walls and most trees are drawn in where the map is silent. His bungalow is fictional, on a real plot.
