At an outdoor lesson today a boy wrote a calculation on his slate. The teacher crossed out the result without giving a reason. I think of the Gymnasium, and of what education ought to do: not merely to transmit results, but to form the habit of examining grounds. A correction without reason may be efficient, but it leaves the mind passive. I do not say the teacher was wrong; I only say that the moment deserved a word. Perhaps the boy should have been asked to show his steps, or perhaps the teacher had a reason I could not hear. In any case, the silence was instructive.

I hope you are well, and that your work is not reduced to the copying of numbers. Your father

Karl,

At an outdoor lesson here, a boy wrote a calculation on his slate. The teacher crossed out the result without giving a reason. I do not say this to condemn the teacher; I only wonder what a boy carries away when he learns that a result is wrong simply because it is declared so. Tell me how you are, and whether you have time for a letter. Your father remains, with affection, your father.
