## 1. Public Premises Act, 1971

**Estate officer.** s.2(b): "an officer appointed as such by the Central Government under section 3"; s.3: gazetted officers of Government. Text via mirror: https://lawgist.in/public-premises-eviction-of-unauthorised-occupants-act/2 and /3. Draft's Estate Officer: **as drafted**. Note: in Delhi he is a gazetted Directorate of Estates officer, not a judge. The GPRA Rules name no separate "Estate Officer"; Rules 62, 64, 69 only refer to the Act.

**s.4 show-cause notice.** Text via mirror, https://courtbook.in/public-premises-eviction-of-unauthorised-occupants-act-1971/section-4: notice states the grounds; the date for showing cause is "not later than seven days" from issue (Act 2 of 2015 replaced "not earlier than"); the person appears with evidence "and also for personal hearing, if such hearing is desired". Draft: a hearing "ten days on" is **wrong: s.4 allows at most seven days, since 2015**. The personal hearing is on request ("if desired"), not automatic. A written notice, then a hearing on the date named: **as drafted** in shape.

**s.5 eviction order.** Text via mirror, https://lawgist.in/public-premises-eviction-of-unauthorised-occupants-act/5: vacate "not later than fifteen days from the date of the order", with a proviso for a further fifteen for compelling reasons recorded in writing; force by the estate officer or his authorised officer. Draft says "thirty days" (part 3, row 2): **wrong: fifteen days (plus fifteen on recorded reasons)**. Thirty is a plain-rent-notice number, not the Act's.

**s.3B (2019).** Exists, inserted by Act 36 of 2019, in force 15 September 2019 (footnote on mirror). https://courtbook.in/public-premises-eviction-of-unauthorised-occupants-act-1971/section-3B. "Notwithstanding anything in section 4 or 5", where the estate officer has information that a person "who was granted residential accommodation occupation" is in unauthorised occupation, he issues notice forthwith to show cause "within three working days"; the notice may be affixed to the outer door and is then deemed served; he considers the cause, records reasons, orders eviction. Force allowed. s.7(3A): a person who challenges a 3B order in court pays damages every month. Would it apply here? By its words it fits a person given occupation by the Government, whose allotment is cancelled or has run out. Our man was never granted anything; the allotment is Hegde's. My reading: the Directorate could use 3B anyway (the practice is to use it for GPRA houses), but the plain s.4 route (seven days, hearing) is the safer fiction, and the draft is **as drafted** in choosing s.4. If Welt wants speed, 3B gives three working days, no hearing right. Reading of applicability is mine, not found stated anywhere.

**s.7 damages.** Via mirror: the estate officer may assess damages for use and occupation under prescribed principles, after a show-cause notice and considering objections; compound interest on arrears (s.7(2A)). https://lawgist.in/public-premises-eviction-of-unauthorised-occupants-act/7. Draft: "assessed monthly": **as drafted**, but the order is made after a notice, and is itself appealable.

**s.9 appeal.** https://courtbook.in/public-premises-eviction-of-unauthorised-occupants-act-1971/section-9: to "the district judge of the district" (or a judicial officer of ten years' standing he designates); **within twelve days** (from publication of the s.5 order; from communication for s.7). The district judge aims to decide in one month, and may stay. Draft: "an appeal within a fixed number of days", carrying the file into March: **as drafted** if twelve days; but the appeal is to a court, not up the Directorate, and not "into March" unless the district judge is slow.

## 2. GPRA Rules, 2017 (text via mirror: https://www.legitquest.com/act/central-government-general-pool-residential-accommodation-rules-2017/8EE8)

**Sharing.** I found no rule allowing sharing of a house with a non-relative. r.66(1): the allottee resides "with his family and immediate relations"; r.66(4): intimation of relations, and of guests staying beyond fifteen days; r.2(v), 67(1): subletting is letting to anyone outside family and immediate relations, and is barred. The only exception is a caretaker relation on leave, up to six months. A Directorate FAQ (summary only, older rules) says sharing without prior intimation counts as subletting. So the draft's "sharing, with the allottee's consent" and "a share of the licence fee each month": **wrong: not found in the 2017 Rules; the nearest thing is subletting, which is penalised**. Sharing between two government servants was an older-rules matter; I did not find it here. If Welt keeps it, the lodger-guest should be called a "guest, intimated beyond fifteen days", and money paid to the allottee is exactly what r.67 forbids.

**Damages.** r.63: after cancellation the occupant "shall be liable to pay damages for use and occupation", plus services, furniture and garden charges, as the Government determines. r.2(d): damages are "a compensation to be levied in multiples of licence fee". Rates are not in the Rules. From a summary (gconnect.in, an OM of 2016): Delhi Type VII and VIII at 55 times the licence fee, others 40 to 50, and "telescopic" (rising each month after the first, up to five times the first month's rate). Current rates not read from the Directorate. Draft: **as drafted** in kind; the sum will be large, not "small": the draft's "can be small" is **wrong** for a Type VII/VIII house.

**Entitlement.** r.8 Table I: Type VI-A level 14, VI-B level 15, VII level 16 (basic Rs 75,000 to 79,999), VIII levels 17 and 18 (Rs 80,000 and over). A Joint Secretary is not named; he sits at level 14, which I infer is Type VI (inference, not stated). Tughlak Road: a mirror of Wikipedia calls 12 Tughlaq Road Type VII (summary only). So the premise of an out-of-entitlement allotment is **plausible, not found stated**; do not give Hegde a Type VII house unless he is a level 16 officer.

**Out-of-turn or special allotment.** r.33 is out-of-turn allotment to personal staff of named dignitaries, not to outsiders; r.83 allows relaxation for reasons recorded in writing. For artists: the "eminent artistes" quota (Cabinet Committee on Accommodation, 1985; Ministry of Culture recommends, Directorate of Estates allots, three years then review; income ceiling; summary only: https://theprint.in/india/as-criticism-over-eviction-mounts-centre-says-govt-accommodation-for-artistes-subject-to-conditions/935406). Draft's "visiting scholar, six months, at a licence fee": **not found**; no scholar category was found. Nearest is the eminent-artistes quota, recommended by the Ministry of Culture, not by the Estate Officer. Estate Officers also do not allot.

**Who handles the file.** r.2(g): "Director of Estates" includes Additional, Deputy and Assistant Directors. Type VII and VIII are allotted by the Minister (summary, esampada FAQ via search). Section officer, Assistant Director, Deputy Director: **as drafted** (the section officer is the natural "Mr. Saxena"; this is my knowledge, not a read text).

**Rules appeal (r.69).** Applies to subletting, thirty days, Deputy Director (enquiry) deciding, Director of Estates hearing appeal. Different from the 1971 Act appeal; the fiction could use either.

## What the draft should change

1. Hearing date: not ten days; s.4 caps the show-cause date at seven days from issue (since 2015). Hearing is "if desired".
2. Vacate in fifteen days (s.5), not thirty; a second fifteen on recorded reasons.
3. Appeal: twelve days, to the district judge, not the Directorate.
4. Sharing with a non-relative and a licence-fee share are not in the 2017 Rules; they read as subletting. Recast the first order (Hegde's guest) or call it a guest intimated beyond fifteen days, with no payment.
5. Damages are multiples of the licence fee (about 50 times for a Type VII or VIII house, telescopic), so not small.
6. "Visiting scholar" term: no such category found; use the eminent-artistes route or drop it.
7. Consider s.3B (three working days, no hearing) as the harsher road; its applicability to a never-allotted occupant is my reading.
