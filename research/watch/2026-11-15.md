# Literature watch, 15 November 2026

Papers first submitted 31 October to 15 November. 41 candidates from 33 queries; 11 kept. A run outside the Friday rhythm, made for the plan of the world's second phase (`WORLD-2.md`). Abstracts were read, not papers; all are unreplicated preprints.

## Kept

**Memory: keep everything, decide at reading time.**

- **DISCERN: Preserve at Write, Resolve at Read for Persistent LLM-Agent Memory.** 11 Nov. [arXiv 2611.15729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15729). On a synthetic memory benchmark a plain retrieval reader already matches the memory systems on recall. What stays unsolved is resolution: which entity is meant, which value is current, and whether to answer at all. Their store only ever adds: it never merges two entities and never drops a superseded value. *Promising.* **Bears on:** A, T41, G2. **Changes:** it confirms the day files as the archive. T41's work belongs in the read path, in saying which fact is current; a "not in the record" answer should count as right in T41's forty questions where the record holds nothing.
- **When Agents Need to Consolidate Memories.** 11 Nov. [arXiv 2611.15644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15644). Under a tight output budget a simple rule that prefers current, authoritative decisions recalled as many of them as the full system; reserving a fifth of the budget for conflicts brought the conflicts back at a small cost in recall. One author, synthetic fixtures, by its own account an engineering analysis. *Promising.* **Bears on:** A, T41. **Changes:** T41's 850 characters could hold a fixed slice for open obligations and corrections. Worth one arm.
- **EvoMemState: Evolving Memory State for Personal Agents.** 2 Nov. [arXiv 2611.03743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03743). Facts carry a state beside them: whether they still hold, in what scope, and how they relate to later facts. *Promising.* **Bears on:** A, the manual's §4.2. **Changes:** nothing. It is the manual's belief and obligation records, argued from another side. Its learned policy is not for us.

**Character: decisions, not diction.**

- **MemRole: From Memories to Decisions for Long-Horizon Reasoning in Role-Playing Agents.** 1 Nov. [arXiv 2611.01778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01778). Role-play models imitate a style well and decide in character badly. A memory pool cut to what the character could know at that point of the story, a fixed order of reasoning (the situation, the memories it wakes, the response), and training on a 32B model give 15 points. *Promising.* **Bears on:** B, what he may know, training. **Changes:** nothing now. *Judgment:* their pool is our 1831 gate. Their gain comes from reference answers in novels, which our data rule has no equivalent of.
- **ACTOR: Action-Centered Temporal Organization for Literary Role-Playing Agents.** 2 Nov. [arXiv 2611.03184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03184). A character's acts, not only its lines, cut from 143 novels in story order, each paired with a plausible act that is out of character. Models choose badly among four next acts; training on the pairs helps (DPO with a likelihood term on the real act). *Promising.* **Bears on:** the Paris backtest, training. **Changes:** a cheap instrument for T50: from the letters, the day so far and four next acts, one of them his. It measures what he does without asking for prose. If a decision adapter is ever reopened, the likelihood term is the documented guard against preference training that forgets how to write.
- **Measuring Model-Specific Alignment Drift in LLM-Based Opinion Dynamics.** 2 Nov. [arXiv 2611.03980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03980). A persona committed to a side keeps it for 20 rounds when left alone, in all five models tested. It drifts in discussion, and how much depends on the model. *Promising.* **Bears on:** D, T8, G4. **Changes:** G4 should ask each question twice, alone and after a conversation. His theses move at night, in the owl, where nobody argues with him; by this paper the rule is the suspect, not the company. A change of model is a change of drift: rerun G4 on any switch.

**Worlds.**

- **GARP: Real-Time Generative Agents on Local Hardware in a Game Engine.** 8 Nov. [arXiv 2611.11064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11064). The whole generative-agents loop on a local 3B model: six needs, relationships updated after every conversation, 50 possible actions narrowed to three to eight before the model is asked, answers held to a schema. 25 of 27 scripted runs pass checks on the logs. *Promising*, a platform paper. **Bears on:** projects like ours. **Changes:** nothing. It supports relationships as state the engine keeps, and narrowing what is offered before the call.
- **Creating Hybrid Interactive Narrative Systems with LLM.** 2 Nov. [arXiv 2611.02807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02807). A shared symbolic world of facts and operations, each operation with preconditions and effects, so that any session can be read as a plan and scored for the player's agency, the manager's control, the author's intent and robustness. No manager wins on all four. *Promising.* **Bears on:** I, world design. **Changes:** it gives the storylines of `WORLD-2.md` their form, and it names what our no-steering rule protects: agency, as a number.
- **Recursive Self-Improvement for Long-Horizon Role-Playing Dialogue.** 5 Nov. [arXiv 2611.07469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07469). An updater that revises a character's memory, instructions and prompt-building policy, written down as a versioned policy with inputs, editable fields, acceptance gates and stopping conditions. Bad replies fall from 14.0% to 4.9% on the authors' own test. *Promising*, internal evaluation only. **Bears on:** the ratchet. **Changes:** nothing. It is our ratchet, automated; its four parts are the checklist for any tuning loop of ours.

**Judging with few judges.**

- **AGORA: Reliability-Aware Aggregation of LLM Judges on Small Evaluation Sets.** 3 Nov. [arXiv 2611.05556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05556). With a few hundred items or fewer, an item-response model that learns each judge's sensitivity from all items beats averaging and Dawid–Skene. *Promising.* **Bears on:** G.
- **Prediction-Powered LLM Ranking with Adaptively Collected Human Feedback.** 9 Nov. [arXiv 2611.13186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13186). A few human judgments and many machine ones combined into estimates with valid intervals, also when the human ones were gathered adaptively. *Promising*, semi-synthetic. **Bears on:** G. **Changes, for both:** a way to close more than one experiment a week. Welt's few pairs calibrate the machine judges (Claude blind, plain Qwen as coder), and the result carries an interval instead of a bare mean.

## Read, not proposed

- **Route to Remember: Adaptive Memory Selection for LLM Agents.** 3 Nov. [arXiv 2611.05636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05636). Facts moved into the weights by self-distillation, with a router between weights and text. For us that would be training every night on his own history: a hard limit, and the manual's §8 already says why not.

## Missed by the search, found the same day

Found by the sweeps for `WORLD-2.md`, inside this window:

- **Replay Fidelity in LLM Agent Evaluation.** Liu and Lu, 8 Nov. [arXiv 2611.11216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11216). A replayed agent run matches the original only where randomness and hidden state are rebuilt and controlled; four replays agreed in output and verdict and still differed in the state they left behind. *Promising*, one benchmark. **Bears on:** the ratchet's frozen days. **Changes:** it supports what T49 found the hard way (152 of 156 cases had different prompts across seeds): compare forks from one saved morning, with the state checked, not replays of a day.
- **Compile as Hypothesis.** Yang and Li, 31 Oct. [arXiv 2611.01135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01135). Prompt optimisers gave no significant gain on strong reasoning models across eight tasks. Where one did gain 0.14, about a quarter of it was the judge's rubric rebuilt inside the prompt. *Promising.* **Bears on:** any search over world text. **Changes:** a tuning loop's confirmation cases are used once, and their judge's rubric never enters the loop.
- **Masked Erosion.** So et al., 28 Oct, [arXiv 2610.38392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38392); and **Evidence-Governed Adoption**, Lonkar, 12 Nov, [arXiv 2611.16378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16378). An acceptance gate sees only what a rule does while the others work, so a rule whose effect another one covers can be deleted without a trace; larger batches made it worse. The second paper answers with fresh paired audits, a declared list of protected abilities tested for no loss, and a small first deployment. *Promising*; the second has no experiment on a real agent. **Bears on:** the ratchet. **Changes:** after a bundle is adopted, switch each older world rule off once, alone, in rehearsal, and see what it was holding.

Five more from this window are in `research/WORLD-2-SOURCES.md` with what they change: MindMem ([arXiv 2611.16223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16223), relationships as state linked to sources); *Knowledge in the Harness, Reliability in the Weights* ([arXiv 2611.15268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15268), rules first, weights for reliability); *Wrong the Same Way* ([arXiv 2611.13218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13218), training on a verifier's reward learns the verifier's errors); *LLMs Don't Know What They Know* ([arXiv 2611.06808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06808), a prompted cutoff is not a cutoff); and a paper on repeated names in generated fiction ([arXiv 2611.01284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01284), a counter at run time against a fixed ban list).
