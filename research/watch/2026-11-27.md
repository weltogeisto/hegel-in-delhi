# Literature watch, 27 November 2026

Papers first submitted 18 to 27 November. 28 candidates from 37 queries; 7 kept. One was read in full because it would change a proposed experiment; the other six are known from their abstracts only.

## Kept, and read in full

- **Measuring and Teaching Language Model Agents to Act on Delayed Consequences.** 19 Nov. [arXiv 2611.28158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28158).
  - **Setup.** SMB Bench is a 90-day wholesale business, with cash, stock and customers who quietly order less weeks after a short delivery. 16 frontier models, 859 runs.
  - **Frontier results.** Only three beat a fixed buying rule. Across 323 quarters with such customers, models linked falling orders to their own shortfalls in writing in 69 runs (21.4%). They protected all six accounts within three days in only 12 (3.7%). One model wrote the link in 26 runs and acted in time in none.
  - **Training our model.** Qwen3.8-27B, with a rank-32 LoRA trained on rules the researchers drew from training businesses and on archived frontier quarters as starting states:
    - its written recognition stayed where it was (85% against 86%);
    - its actions changed: protection reversals fell from 5.8 to 1.0 a quarter, and quarters losing more than $10K from 25% to 8%;
    - the profit gain on the one sealed test business is uncertain (+$1,489 ± $1,133).

  *Promising*; one test business, with rules extracted by the researchers. **Bears on:** A, B, gap 1, T41, T43, G4, G9. **Changes:** T41's 20 decision contexts score two things apart: whether his thought names the outcome or obligation in play, and whether his action follows from it. Only the second counts for T41's bar.

  *Judgment:*
  - This is the gap between his commitments and his choices (2.0–2.8 against 4.65) seen from another side. Writing that he must pay Ramesh is not paying him.
  - The owl's revision log can diagnose without anything changing the next day, so G4's probe should look at choices as well as answers.
  - Their training method does not fit our data rule, since it uses frontier models' runs. That it moved actions and not words is the part worth knowing.

## Known from the abstract only

- **Admitted Is Not Effective: Lessons from a Notary-Gated Retrospective Loop.** 22 Nov. [arXiv 2611.29727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29727). A pre-registered study of 880 pull requests:
  - an agent proposed rule edits every ten, and a notary admitted 55 of 89 and rejected 8 that cited evidence that did not exist;
  - held-out outcomes did not improve after ten rounds;
  - the authors conclude that every learned rule should be admitted, and retired, as a testable hypothesis.

  **Bears on:** G, the ratchet, T42. **Changes:** nothing. *Judgment:* it supports the ratchet's insistence on frozen held-out inputs. It also restates the report's caution about T42: a gate that checks the cited evidence admits a change; it does not show the change did any good.
- **Epistemic Guard.** 20 Nov. [arXiv 2611.27095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27095). It names two failures:
  - "action fabrication": prose narrating side effects that failed or never ran;
  - "provenance collapse": unverified prose entering durable memory beside real results.

  It checks the prose of each turn against signed receipts with small encoders on the CPU, 279 MB in all and no GPU. On 250 frozen traces it reached 93.2%, ahead of 12–14B judges on a GPU. **Bears on:** F, G1, G8. **Changes:** nothing yet. *Judgment:* "action fabrication" is the tailor's order called ready, and G8 currently scores the prose by hand. A checker on the Pi's CPU is a later possibility, worth reading in full before anyone proposes it.
- **Lazy Agents Answer Against Their Own Beliefs.** 24 Nov. [arXiv 2611.32884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32884). Agents commit to an answer while their own written belief still names several. A reward computed from the simulator's exact posterior teaches them to wait for enough evidence: premature commitment fell from 3–68% of such turns to 0.1–2.2%. Vision-language models on puzzles; trained. **Bears on:** B, D. **Changes:** nothing. Like the paper above, it finds stated belief and action apart.
- **Legal Is Not Good.** 21 Nov. [arXiv 2611.28713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28713). A pre-registered strategy game. Small open models (3B, 8B), constrained to legal moves, never played an illegal one and still lost to a random player; 7–8B models served locally behaved the same. **Bears on:** F, the Act 1 gates. **Changes:** nothing. *Judgment:* the world's rules make his choices legal, not good for him. The gates count refusals and quiet steps, which is legality and liveliness; nothing yet measures whether his choices serve him.
- **One Model, Many Modes.** 25 Nov. [arXiv 2611.34760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34760). Sampling one of several evolved system prompts per answer raised diversity (1.92 to 3.43) and novelty (1.00 to 3.34) at some cost in quality (3.72 to 3.30), all judged by an LLM. **Bears on:** E, G6. **Changes:** nothing. *Judgment:* varying the persona prompt per step would trade his consistency for variety. G6 varies the plan, not the man, and stays the safer route.
- **How a Prediction Dies.** 20 Nov. [arXiv 2611.28371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28371). One claim on a multi-agent platform with an append-only ledger, from registration to refutation. Its criterion became executable only under pressure from the community. Its author, bound by the clause he registered, refuted it. One case. **Bears on:** D, T42. **Changes:** nothing. *Judgment:* a thesis that states what would shake it is easier to test. Hegel's own account (the research note's H11) lets that criterion itself be revised.
