# Hegel in Delhi: why training isn't advancing, and what would

A brief for GPT 6 Pro, 6 November 2026. Please think about this deeply and critically. We want a diagnosis and a plan, not reassurance. Your answer is advice only: per the project's rule (below), you must not write training data, example thoughts or texts meant to go into training.

## The project in one paragraph

G. W. F. Hegel, with knowledge up to his death in 1831, wakes in Delhi in 2026 and lives there day by day in a simulated world.
- **The world:** a rule engine on a Raspberry Pi, with places, prices, money, meals, weather and people who speak to him. It checks every decision against its rules, refuses invalid ones with reasons, and keeps a record.
- **His mind:** a local model, Qwen3.8-27B (UD-Q4_K_M GGUF), served by llama.cpp on one RTX 3090 (24 GB) in a Windows/WSL PC that runs about 12 hours a day.
- **His day:** 09:00 to 21:00 Berlin. Every 15 to 30 minutes he decides in JSON: a thought, an action, a place, minutes, what he says and what he buys. The prompt (the soul, the rendered situation, recent thoughts, passages from his own translated works) runs to about 5,000 to 7,000 tokens. He makes 25 to 40 decisions a day.
- **The owl:** writes up each day at 09:10.

It has run privately (the "shadow world") since 12 October and is on Day 37.

**What Welt, the owner, wants:** a Hegel whose days get better. He doesn't loop, stays true to what the world recorded, sounds like Hegel, and develops convictions grounded in evidence. A public version comes later.

## Hard constraints

- **The data rule.** Training data comes only from:
  - Hegel's own public-domain texts (English translations: Sibree, Wallace, Baillie, Dyde and others; German *Rechtsphilosophie*);
  - the local model's own outputs, selected by fixed deterministic rules such as the world's checks;
  - Welt's own judgement.

  Never text written or selected by Claude, GPT or any frontier model.
- **Hardware:** one RTX 3090. Hegel needs the card during his day unless he is paused. Any spending, such as a cloud GPU, is Welt's decision. You may recommend it, with costs.
- **Stack:** Unsloth (4-bit QLoRA), TRL and llama.cpp, which applies a LoRA at a per-request scale.

## What has been tried, with numbers

**1. The "Hegelizer" pilot (October).** A restyle LoRA turns a plain version of a thought into the manner of Hegel's English translators. It was trained on 186 pairs: a Qwen paraphrase of a book passage as input, the translator's passage as target.
- On reconstruction it reached Burrows' Delta 0.34 against the translators' books (real passages 0.33, prompted Qwen 0.43).
- It copied: 12 of 80 outputs shared 8-word runs with the source.

**2. The Hegelizer, round two (6 November).**
- **Data:** 12,750 pairs from 6,375 units, with two plain versions each, generated by Qwen with a diversity filter. The held-out units came from separate chapters.
- **Training:** the goldfish loss; LoRA rank 16, alpha 32, learning rate 2e-4, effective batch 8; 517 steps (one capped epoch), with an evaluation every 100 steps. Checkpoint 400 was chosen by machine numbers.
- **The test:** 40 of his real thoughts, restyled, against prompted Qwen:

| KPI | Bar | Adapter | Prompted Qwen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delta against the translators' books | at most 0.38 (set from book passages: real 0.33, plain 0.48) | **0.7644** | 0.7600 |
| Meaning kept (plain-Qwen judge: says everything, adds nothing) | at least 36/40 | **23/40** | 34/40 |
| Copying (12-word runs from any book) | at most 1 | 0 ✓ | |
| Live contract (valid, leaks, time) | 20/20, 0, at most 8 s | 20/20, 0, 4.3 s ✓ | |

The pilot adapter, at scales 1.0, 0.8 and 0.6, also failed the automatic gates. A blind human preference test (40 pairs) is pending but can't change the verdict.

**Our hypotheses, unverified:**
- **(a) A domain gap.** The targets are 19th-century philosophy. The test inputs are thoughts about a caretaker's unpaid wage, an empty gas cylinder and an estates clerk, and no Hegel text exists about those.
- **(b) A miscalibrated bar.** The Delta bar was set on book passages and may be unreachable for short, modern-topic thoughts.
- **(c) Learned content.** The adapter learned to produce translator content, hence the meaning loss.
- **(d) A noisy judge.** The meaning judge may be noisy.

**3. Repetition.** The share of his thoughts that reuse a 4-word phrase from earlier the same day is 67–69% on full days (Days 12 and 27) and 48% in rehearsals. The top phrases are "the friction of" and "the system of needs". The world refuses near-verbatim repeats, which causes refusal loops; on Day 27 he wrote the same thought three times. Yesterday evening he wrote "the friction of the particular against the universal" twice in 25 minutes.

**4. Truth.** Typed records in the prompt cut contradictions from 3 to 1 and statements-as-fact from 7 to 2 in a smoke test. Voices' claims still get written up by the owl as fact. He also states world facts wrongly: "the sun has set" at 16:37, with sunset given as 17:33.

**5. Operations.** One training run took four attempts over three days. Each failure was in tooling:
- a GPU admission check counted the trainer itself;
- Unsloth's `generate` left gradient checkpointing off after an evaluation, so the memory spilled and steps took 15 times longer;
- a supervisor path bug;
- a VRAM gate set too low.

The throughput: a 27B QLoRA step (8 sequences of up to 1,024 tokens) takes 20–30 seconds, and 20 held-out generations take about 21 minutes. **Each experiment costs about a day.**

## What is planned now

- **T39, running now:** inference only, three arms on 157 frozen decision cases with 3 seeds each.
  - **A:** as is.
  - **B:** an echo guard, which shows him "already said today" phrases.
  - **C:** llama.cpp's DRY sampler at 0.8.

  It measures the echo share, the truth audit, and blind argument and voice scores.
- **Training 3, prepared:** DPO for his decision calls on pairs labelled by `World.check` plus the phrase rule.
  - **Chosen:** accepted and repeats no phrase. **Rejected:** refused or repeating.
  - One pair per situation, length-matched within 20%.
  - Only 118 real situations exist outside the test days, so new rehearsal days generated by the world fill it to about 500.
  - Prompts run to 5–7K tokens; LoRA rank 16.
  - It must beat T39's better arm.
- **Later:** typed facts for the people he meets (T40), then grounded voices by preference training (Training 2).

## What we ask you

1. **Diagnose round two.** Which hypothesis do you believe, and what cheap test would separate them? Is Delta a valid voice metric for short modern thoughts at all, and what would be? Is a separate restyle adapter the right architecture, against these alternatives:
   - continued pretraining of the decision model on his texts;
   - retrieval with exemplars in the prompt (his shelf already does a version of this);
   - activation steering;
   - leaving the voice to prompting?
2. **Where can training beat inference-time fixes** on one 3090, within the data rule? Rank the candidates: decisions, repetition, truthfulness, the voices' grounding, voice, and convictions.
3. **Critique Training 3.**
   - Are about 500 world-labelled pairs with 6K-token prompts enough?
   - Which method: DPO, KTO (unpaired, so no pairing loss), ORPO, SimPO, rejection-sampling fine-tuning (STaR or RFT style), or context distillation of the echo guard (training him to behave as if the guard were in the prompt)?
   - What are the expected effect sizes and failure modes (verbosity, mode collapse, gaming the world's checks, losing voice)?
   - Should it be iterated on-policy?
4. **Speed up the loop.** How do we get from about a day per experiment to hours?
   - Recipes on a smaller proxy model (Qwen 8B or 14B) first;
   - shorter or cheaper evaluations, or proxies that predict the expensive ones;
   - caching;
   - or a rented GPU for a few hours, with costs.
5. **The base model.** Is a 27B 4-bit model on a 3090 the right base for training at all? A side trial found Swift 1.5 about equal in quality and about 20% faster.
6. **What are we missing structurally?** For example: should "advance" be measured by outcomes in the world (fewer refusals, more varied days, convictions that change on evidence) rather than by voice metrics?
