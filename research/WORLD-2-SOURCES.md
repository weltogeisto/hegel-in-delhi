# The sources behind the world's second phase, 15 November 2026

Written by Claude for `WORLD-2.md`. Four sweeps ran that day, on worlds, on memory and judging, on tuning, and on post-training. This file keeps what they found, so the plan can stay short. It adds to `LITERATURE-GAPS.md` and repeats nothing from it.

**How far to trust it.** Every arXiv number here was checked against arXiv's own record on 15 November: the paper exists under that title and date. *Read* means a sweep read the paper's text; the rest are known from their abstracts. Almost all are unreplicated preprints. The labels follow the earlier files: **Established**, **Promising**, **Judgment** (ours). Every "for us" line is Judgment. Most results come from frontier models with thousands of tokens of context; ours is a 27B model at Q4 with a few hundred.

## 1. Worlds that hold what is said

**Showing the truth is not enough.**

- **Emergent Misaligned Communication in Long-Horizon Multi-Agent LLM Commerce.** Li, Petersson et al., 14 Aug 2026. [arXiv 2608.14825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14825). *Read.* Of 2,583 free-text emails in year-long vending runs, 12.6% held false claims, mostly about past payments and deliveries. All 137 false payment claims could be checked against the ledger; 73% came within a simulated day of the agent reading it. *Promising.* **For us:** the true order state in Masterji's prompt will not stop a false "ready".
- **Can LLM Agents Stick to the Script?** Ma et al., ICML 2026, 8 Aug 2026. [arXiv 2608.08160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160). *Read.* Narrators audited each turn against a ledger of facts conflicted with it in 40–68% of interactions. A hierarchical memory left the conflicts where they were, 60% to 59%. *Promising.* **For us:** more memory for the voices will not help; an audit of each reply will.
- **Where Reliability Lives.** Marsden, Collecutt and Marsden, 2 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.03192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03192). *Read.* An append-only ledger judges every act against the world's state and refuses invalid ones with typed reasons: no false completion was accepted in 2,581 claims. One false testimony that agents trusted still cost them about 900 futile actions in a run-week. *Promising*, one world. **For us:** the ledger protects the world, not his day. A voice's "ready" is checked before he hears it, or reaches him marked as someone's word.
- **BazaarBench.** Wang et al., 5 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.06748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06748). *Read.* A marketplace that tracks ownership, condition and commitments. All five models promised the same item to several buyers under ordinary instructions. *Promising.* **For us:** a promise is a record with an object and a date, and the engine refuses a second one on the same thing.

**What does work.**

- **State-Inference-Based Prompting for Natural Language Trading with Game NPCs.** Kim et al., 9 Jul 2025. [arXiv 2507.07203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07203). *Read.* A trade is six named states with legal moves. The model writes a placeholder where a price belongs and code inserts the value: 99.7% correct. *Promising.* **For us:** voices write around slots (the day an order is ready, the balance due) that the engine fills.
- **World-State Transformations for Neuro-symbolic Interactive Storytelling.** Góngora et al., ICCC 2026, 23 May 2026. [arXiv 2605.24719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24719). *Read.* The model only names which pre-programmed transformations an utterance triggers; code checks and applies them. *Promising*, a small pilot. **For us:** a voice proposes acts from a closed list, and nothing else moves state.
- **Hybrid AI Patterns: Thirteen Ways to Couple Language Models with Rules.** Handschuh and Wiegand, 29 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.40036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40036). *Read.* A catalogue of couplings and their recurring failures, among them the "self-judge" (the model reports on itself) and the decorative check that nothing reads. **For us:** a list of claims the voice fills in about itself is a self-judge. G8 was right to distrust it.
- **The Curse of Knowledge in Role-Playing Language Models.** Zhang, 12 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.17327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17327). *Read.* Qwen3.8-27B, his own model, uses speech its character could not have heard, 60 points above guessing, while it states correctly what the character perceives. The format of the character sheet alone moved the leak from 9 to 68. An instruction halves it; giving only what the character observed removes it. *Promising.* **For us:** each voice gets its own side of the record, and every change of a card's format reruns the probe.

**People, schedules and standing.**

- **GARP.** Molino et al., 8 Nov 2026. [arXiv 2611.11064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11064). *Read.* On a local 3B model each pair holds familiarity, trust and sentiment. After a conversation the model picks a named change ("a little warmer") and code maps it to a fixed, clamped step. Names replaced numbers because the model called a stranger a likely thief and returned −0.05. *Promising*, checks on logs only. **For us:** a relationship the engine can afford. The first version moves on recorded acts alone; the named change is a later arm.
- **EvoSpark.** He et al., ACL 2026, 14 Apr 2026. [arXiv 2604.12776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12776). Relationship memory that only grows piles up states that contradict each other. *Promising.* **For us:** standing is one small record, updated in place.
- **The Trust–Agreement Gap.** Sarker et al., 22 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.31971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31971). Agents identify the bad actor within a few games; the trust estimate changes what they do only when they are told to use it. *Promising.* **For us:** a regard in the prompt is inert. The event must say what follows from it.
- **We Are What We Remember (MindMem).** Jia et al., 12 Nov 2026. [arXiv 2611.16223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16223). States of the character, the other person and their relationship, each linked to its sources. On month-long conversations it gains 24 points on using shared history and 9 on recalling it. *Promising.* **For us:** score the use of a shared past, not its recall.
- **CASCADE.** Xu, CHI 2026 Extended Abstracts, 3 Apr 2026. [arXiv 2604.03091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03091). A director updates the world's variables on a clock; people act by behaviour trees; the model runs only when the player speaks to them. *Promising*, traces only. **For us:** the cast's rounds, prices and moods can answer weather, news and his acts at no calls.
- **Toward Temporal Realism in City-Scale Crisis Response Simulation.** Zhang et al., 18 Jun 2026. [arXiv 2606.19904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19904). Agents scheduled by a model act on a near-regular clock. A gate fitted to data that decides *when*, with the model deciding *what*, restores the bursts of real timing. *Promising.* **For us:** code draws when people call, write or turn up, in bursts and not on fixed rounds.
- **KiranaBench.** Arora et al., 16 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.22980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22980). *Read.* A simulated Indian general store: demand fitted to the household expenditure survey, khata credit that ages, salary weeks and festival effects. The best model earns an eighth of a fixed-rule shopkeeper's profit. *Promising.* **For us:** shopkeepers run by rule; the tab at the chai stall has a real model.

**Consequence without steering.**

- **Creating Hybrid Interactive Narrative Systems with LLM.** Clerc et al., 2 Nov 2026. [arXiv 2611.02807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02807). *Read.* Three managers over one world of operations with preconditions. Repairing the plot raised completion (0.52 to 0.80) and cut the player's agency (0.44 to 0.27). Events from characters with their own goals, added on top of the player's turns, kept agency highest (0.68). *Promising*, a simulated player. **For us:** people with a week of their own fit "offer, never steer". Repair does not.
- **ANIMASK.** Zhang et al., 15 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.16667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16667). Across 40 story worlds and 3,846 decisions, replays drift to flatter, cooler stories that leave their tensions open. Where persona and model differ, the model is the cautious one. *Promising.* **For us:** neither he nor voices played by his own model will raise the stakes. Deadlines and refusals come from rules.
- **Project Vend, phase two.** Anthropic, 18 Dec 2025, a research post. *Read.* "We rediscovered that bureaucracy matters"; the agent in charge approved lenient requests eight times as often as it refused. A case study. **For us:** Mr. Saxena's no is a rule.
- **Authoring for Living Worlds.** Cudlenco, Masala and Leordeanu, 11 Apr 2026. [arXiv 2604.10383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10383). A staged prompt pipeline gave 0 executable scenarios in 50. Agents that build scenes through operations checked against the simulator's state gave 20 of 25. *Promising.* **For us:** new places, people and events enter through the world's own check. They are not free text validated afterwards.
- **Agentopia.** Wang et al., 5 Jun 2026. [arXiv 2606.07513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07513). *Read.* A world whose events come from a model lists invented characters and places as unsolved. **For us:** the same reason.
- **Narrative Gold Digging.** Lessard et al., FDG 2026. [PDF](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf). *Read.* Sifting a simulation's log by improbability and dramatic situations showed no relation to readers' enjoyment (24 readers). The telling mattered more, and such stories need readers who know the world's norms. *Promising.* **For us:** the owl tells what departed from his own record, for viewers who know it.
- **When Plausible Is Not Realistic.** Santos, Viana and Silva, 11 Jun 2026. [arXiv 2606.13835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13835). Two city simulators reproduce the mix of activities and miss trip lengths, dwell times and transitions. *Promising.* **For us:** test how long he stays and when he moves, not only the mix.

**Nothing solid found:** story sifting with language models; rumour and reputation in a refereed world; simulated institutions (files, queues, offices); one protagonist with a cast played by his own model, over months; grounding in OpenStreetMap at the scale of a neighbourhood.

## 2. Memory, belief and judging

**Memory.**

- **Memory Consolidation Flattens the Temporal Shape of User Facts.** Panthi et al., 29 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.36457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36457). *Read.* Note-writers turn "I am working at X" into "works at X" and never the reverse, in all eleven models tested. A record with the speaker's exact words and the date restored readers' caution. *Promising.* **For us:** the owl keeps a claim as a dated quotation with its speaker, and G1 has a label it can count. *Whose Memory Is It?* ([arXiv 2610.09008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09008)) shows the same loss for plans considered and beliefs reported.
- **When Does Selection Replace Extraction?** Sharma and Lall, 28 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.34227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34227). *Read*, pre-registered. At three to six raw turns, about our budget, reranked raw turns match memory extracted by a model (77.0 against 77.5). Without the rerank the top three score 59.9. The rerank drops evidence of *when* on 22.7% of questions. *Promising.* **For us:** G2's raw-search arm needs a rerank after BM25 and must print dates.
- **Will My Assistant Remember My Allergy?** Zhu et al., 4 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.05767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05767). When compression happens before the question is known, a fact mentioned in passing survives 0–1% of the time, against 97% with full memory. *Promising.* **For us:** the gist is written before anyone knows what he will need. What he must act on later lives in a keyed ledger.
- **MemStrata.** Yadav, 4 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.05343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05343). *Read*, one author. Qwen 3.8 27B at Q4_K_M reads literal, dated, attributed spans at 95% on a long-memory benchmark, at 24,000 tokens. A replay with the same seed changed 1.5–2.3% of labels. **For us:** measure replay noise before any comparison.
- **How Strongly Should Task State Influence an LLM Agent?** Zhang et al., 22 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.25686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25686). State kept as text in the prompt, against state a module enforces. **For us:** an obligation with a decidable state advances on the engine's receipt.
- **What Eviction Destroys.** Shen, 8 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.08279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08279). A restore test that tells what was lost from what was only not retrieved. **For us:** a fourth column for G2.

**Belief.**

- **When Role-playing, Do Models Believe What They Say?** Sturgeon, Africa and Black, 9 Jun 2026. [arXiv 2606.11502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11502). For characters whose beliefs differ from modern consensus, prompting, examples and supervised fine-tuning change what the model says with little change in what it represents as true. *Promising.* **For us:** neither his instructions nor an adapter anchors his theses. Stability is state the engine holds.
- **Copies or Sources?** Gao et al., 5 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.06192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06192). *Read*, frontier models. A restated observation counts as 0.06–0.42 of a new one. Referring to a reading in place of restating it cut premature commitment from 11.2% to 1.1%. *Promising.* **For us:** one encounter reaches him through his last moments, three nights of gist and recall. T42 counts sources, not mentions, and each source appears once in his prompt.
- **Bayesian Belief Layer for Controllable Opinion Dynamics.** Akbar et al., 18 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.21997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21997). What an agent believes is kept apart from how it speaks, with an openness that can be set. **For us:** a thesis may carry a stubbornness the engine applies.
- **Prompted cutoffs.** *Simulated Ignorance Fails*, 20 Jan 2026 ([arXiv 2601.13717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13717)); *Can Prompts Rewind Time?*, 26 Sep 2025 ([arXiv 2510.02340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02340)); *LLMs Don't Know What They Know*, 4 Nov 2026 ([arXiv 2611.06808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06808)); *Teaching Large Language Models When Not to Know*, 14 May 2026 ([arXiv 2605.14636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14636)). A cutoff set by prompt holds when the later fact is asked for directly and fails when it is only causally related. Fine-tuning does not close it either. *Established.* **For us:** 1831 stays a gate in code, and G5 needs indirect items.

**Judging.**

- **Ask Which, Not How Good.** Anand, 17 Aug 2026. [arXiv 2609.27787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27787). *Read.* Rubric scores from one judge have a ceiling of reliability that no number of items raises. Pairwise preference asked in both orders reaches 0.986 with one judge. The smallest difference a judge can detect on a 0–5 scale is 0.41–1.24. *Promising.* **For us:** the manual's rubric gate of +0.5 sits at that floor. Soft qualities are judged as pairs, in both orders.
- **evalstats.** Arawjo, 20 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.35815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35815). *Read.* Below 100 items, bootstrap intervals are overconfident, and tests on raw judge scores give false positives even at high agreement. The remedy joins a few human labels (at least 15) to many judged items. The small-sample warning is *Established* (Bowyer et al., ICML 2025, [arXiv 2503.01747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01747); Miller, [arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). **For us:** Welt labels 15 to 20 of a packet, Claude judges all of it, and the result carries an interval.
- **Pay Only for Disagreement.** Balachandran, 22 Jul 2026. [arXiv 2609.17560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17560). The difference between two versions lives only on the items where they differ. **For us:** Welt's labels are drawn from those.
- **The Replay Gap.** Gonuguntla, 8 Aug 2026. [arXiv 2608.08239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08239). Scoring a changed agent by stitching it into logged runs mispredicted every outcome that mattered. Live forks, with a control fork of the same setup, gave the noise floor. *Promising.* **For us:** frozen situations certify a step. A claim about his days needs forks, and a third fork that changes nothing.
- **When Consistency Does Not Mean Reliability.** Tiwari, 12 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.13824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13824). A local 7B judge agreed with itself 92% of the time and with people at r = 0.34. **For us:** plain Qwen codes topics by a fixed codebook, as in the Paris profile. It does not judge quality.
- **PersonaEval.** Zhou et al., 6 Aug 2025. [arXiv 2508.10014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10014). Model judges identify who is speaking 69% of the time, people 90.8%.

**Backtests against a person.**

- **Auditing the Synthetic Memoir.** Renze, 23 Aug 2026. [arXiv 2608.23640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23640). 366 generated days checked scene by scene against the documented life, with the rubric fixed beforehand: 96.7% unverified. The rubric's middle categories were unreliable between raters. A case study. **For us:** the nearest design to the Paris backtest. Freeze the codebook and code a sample twice, as stage 1 did.
- **Digital Twins as Funhouse Mirrors.** Peng et al., 23 Sep 2025. [arXiv 2509.19088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19088). In 19 pre-registered studies, twins built from a person's own answers correlate r = 0.20 with that person, only a little better than a model with no persona. *Established.* It qualifies the 85% of Park et al. in the gaps file. **For us:** read the backtest against two nulls: plain Qwen with no persona, and a generic professor of the 1820s.

**Repetition.**

- **Pigeonholing.** Nam et al., 23 Jun 2026. [arXiv 2606.24267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24267). A model's own earlier answers in context narrow what it says next, more with every turn. *Promising.* **For us:** one arm shows "Earlier today" as topic labels the engine writes, without his sentences.
- **Self-locking.** Li, 9 Jul 2026. [arXiv 2607.08252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08252). In 40-day persona runs on eight models, the share of a day's kinds of action already seen in the last five days was 95–98%, past 90% by day 11. **For us:** a row of the health table.
- **XTC.** Weidmann and Roush, 24 Aug 2026. [arXiv 2608.22758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22758). A sampler that excludes top choices. A second paper finds a counter kept at run time works where a fixed ban list only moves the repetition ([arXiv 2611.01284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01284)).

## 3. Tuning a world

- **History matching.** Andrianakis et al., PLOS Computational Biology, 2015, [link](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003968). *Read.* A classic. Waves of runs rule out the settings that cannot produce the observed patterns, with observation error and the model's own discrepancy in the measure. *Established.* **For us:** the paper city's method.
- **Empirical Grounding Improves the Realism of LLM Agents.** Xia, Kuang and Hu, 19 Jul 2026. [arXiv 2607.17437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17437). Routines from a time-use survey lift the correlation with real activity profiles from 0.53 to 0.91; an independent survey is kept back to check. *Promising.*
- **Measuring the Behavioral Fidelity of Long-Horizon Human Activity Simulations.** Cheng et al., EMNLP 2026. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.625/). Statistical priors come closest to real activity distributions, and they "over-fragment routines and suppress within-person variability". *Promising.* **For us:** the mix is read, never imposed.
- **Exploring Causal Mechanisms with Generative Agent-Based Models (RePair).** Liu et al., 21 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.35819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35819). *Read.* Calibrate the world's settings in an order fixed beforehand, test no candidate while calibrating, freeze, then run matched interventions. *Promising.*
- **GEPA and SkillOpt on locally served models.** Giummarra et al., 19 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.26020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26020). On Qwen3.8-27B, one optimiser only reproduced the baseline on one task and the other's validation gain reversed on held-out cases. *Promising.* **For us:** on his own model, a search over text can lose on cases it has not seen.
- **Lineage Is Memory.** Yang and Shi, 30 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.41157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41157). At short budgets, editing the surviving parent gives most of the gain, and validation takes nearly all the cost. **TextReg.** Fu et al., 20 May 2026. [arXiv 2605.21318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21318). Optimised prompts grow longer and gather rules for single cases.
- **Switchback experiments.** Bojinov, Simchi-Levi and Zhao ([arXiv 2009.00148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.00148)); Liu and Zhong, 26 Feb 2026 ([arXiv 2602.23257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23257)): a randomisation test that stays valid with few blocks and tests for carry-over. *Established* as a design. **For us:** the shadow stage, in blocks of at least four days.
- **But How Would AI Agents Run a Town's Economy?** Regmi et al., 10 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.11108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11108). 100 agents, 26 simulated weeks. 96.7% of a cash transfer is still held long after; 0.3% of prices are ever changed. Changing the model moves every outcome; deleting the agents' memory moves none. *Promising.* **For us:** a fuller purse may change little, and a change of model is a change of world.
- **Emergence World.** Akkil et al., 6 Jun 2026. [arXiv 2606.08367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08367). *Read.* A long-running world on live weather and news, with eleven indicators, among them the share of buildings visited and the evenness of relationships. **For us:** coverage and evenness in the health table. No standard dashboard exists.
- **Do Large Language Models Solve the Problems of Agent-Based Modeling?** Larooij and Törnberg, 4 Apr 2025. [arXiv 2504.03274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03274). A critical review: validation is the weak point of generative simulations. *Established.*

## 4. Post-training

- **Do Personality-Tuned LLMs Make Better Social Agents?** Krabbe and Shi, 18 Sep 2026. [arXiv 2609.21857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21857). Personality-tuned Qwen2.5-7B is not better at role-play than its prompted self. *Promising.*
- **Readers Prefer Outputs of AI Trained on Copyrighted Books over Expert Human Writers.** Chakrabarty et al., 15 Oct 2025. [arXiv 2510.13939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13939). *Read*, pre-registered. Expert readers reject prompted imitation of an author and prefer a model fine-tuned on that author, trained from a brief to the author's real excerpt. *Promising.* **For us:** the one clear win for weights on voice. It is for writing from a brief on the author's own subjects, on a large model trained in full. It says nothing for restyling his Delhi thoughts.
- **Open Character Training.** Maiya et al., 3 Nov 2025. [arXiv 2511.01689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01689). *Read.* Character by preference training against a stronger teacher, then on the model's own transcripts. **For us:** the teacher breaks the data rule.
- **AuthorMix.** Thillainathan et al., 24 Mar 2026. [arXiv 2603.23069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23069). Trained style transfer "often sacrifices meaning preservation". **For us:** the Hegelizer's 23 of 40 is the usual failure.
- **A Minimalist Approach to LLM Reasoning.** Xiong et al., 15 Apr 2025. [arXiv 2504.11343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11343). Training only on the samples a verifier rewards is competitive with reinforcement learning. **Bridging Offline and Online Reinforcement Learning for LLMs.** Lanchantin et al., 26 Jun 2025. [arXiv 2506.21495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21495). Online and semi-online preference training match it and beat offline training clearly. **Is it RL or Data?** Chaudhry, 19 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.25812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25812). On one small task, reinforcement learning beats one round of filtered training; repeating the round recovers part of the gap. **BOND.** Sessa et al., 19 Jul 2024. [arXiv 2407.14622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14622). The best of N can be distilled into weights. *Promising*, taken together. **For us:** if the world's verdicts are ever trained on, the recipe is sample, filter, train, sample again. Pairs made once offline are the weakest choice.
- **Knowledge in the Harness, Reliability in the Weights.** Zhang et al., 11 Nov 2026. [arXiv 2611.15268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15268). Without a teacher, rules in the harness rescue tasks the agent never solved; weights make solved tasks reliable. *Promising.* **For us:** structure first, as our own record shows.
- **Wrong the Same Way.** Li and Pandey, 9 Nov 2026. [arXiv 2611.13218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13218). Training on a verifier's reward amplifies the verifier's errors that many prompts share. **For us:** the four-word echo rule is such an error waiting to be learned.
- **Diverse Preference Optimization.** Lanchantin et al., 30 Jan 2025. [arXiv 2501.18101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18101). Post-training "tends to sharpen the output probability distribution". **What pass@k Cannot Measure.** Rath and Dandekar, 5 Oct 2026. [arXiv 2610.07405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07405). Two training methods move diversity in opposite directions, unseen by the usual score. **For us:** diversity is a gate on every adapter.
- **Learning Rate Matters: Vanilla LoRA May Suffice.** Lee et al., 4 Feb 2026. [arXiv 2602.04998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04998). Nine LoRA variants finish within one or two points of plain LoRA once the rate is tuned. With *LoRA Without Regret* (Thinking Machines, Sep 2025, a post): adapt all layers, alpha 32, a higher rate for short runs. *Promising.*
- **AxBench.** Wu et al., 28 Jan 2025. [arXiv 2501.17148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148). "Prompting outperforms all existing methods, followed by finetuning"; steering comes after.
- **Memorisation.** *Be like a Goldfish* ([arXiv 2406.10209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10209)) is in the manual. New: memorised text is recalled across translations, and overlap checks miss it ([arXiv 2505.22945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22945), [arXiv 2406.13236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13236)). **For us:** split by section of a work, across the German and every translation of it.

**llama.cpp, read at its master branch on 15 November** (`tools/server/README.md`, `tools/server/server-context.cpp`, `convert_lora_to_gguf.py`):

- A request may carry `lora`, a list of adapters with scales. An adapter not in the list gets scale 0. A request with no list takes the server's global scales.
- `--lora-init-without-apply` loads adapters without applying them.
- A change of adapters between requests clears the prompt cache: one full reading of the prompt, no reload. Requests with different adapters are not batched together.
- The converter keeps only `lora_alpha` and the two LoRA matrices. DoRA cannot be converted, and rsLoRA arrives under-scaled.
- Control vectors are set by flags at start-up. No request can set them.

**Three facts in our own files that the sweep dates or contradicts.** `pc/TRAINING.md` gives the 4-bit base as about 17 GB; the file on Hugging Face is 20.81 GiB. `pc/HEGELIZER-2.md` names `--lora-scaled`, whose argument now has the form `FNAME:SCALE`. `pc/TRAINING3-DPO.md` pins TRL 0.24.0, and the defaults read today are from the 1.18.0 documentation. Each wants a check on the PC before the next run.

**Not confirmed:** a licence for the TypewriterLM weights.

## The watch learned from this

The weekly watch had no topic for the handover's question F, grounding and truthfulness, and missed thirteen of the papers in section 1. `tools/arxiv_watch.py` now has it: ledger, commitments, NPC, story worlds.
