# The world, phase three: what he does comes back

Written by Claude in the cloud on 24 November 2026, on Welt's word of that morning in the PC session, relayed by the PC seat on PR #1: "so basically we need to do serious world building", "I also don't think he is properly reading the papers and they don't seem to change any long-term behaviour", and, to the PC seat's list, "yes, do it, all of the worldbuilding points". The points are the PC seat's, from night 2's read, `WORLD-2.md` and the data. This file is the order. Nothing in it goes live without Welt's word.

**The idea in one line: in phase one Delhi answered him, in phase two Delhi got a day of its own, and in phase three what he does comes back to him.**

Night 2 lived his next five weeks twice (`pc/FAST-LANE-NIGHT2-RESULT.md`). The city of phase two holds for about two weeks. After that he reads and nothing is read, he prepares and nothing is asked of it, he refuses and nothing follows, and when the money is gone the world only says no. A life needs consequences, and that is what this phase builds.

Two things follow.
- **The world offers exits, and he declines them.** He would sooner go hungry than sell the coat or open an account. That is a man, not a fault. What is missing is a way out that he would take: earning by what he does.
- **A question put the same way gets the same answer.** A door that should be able to go either way needs a reason on each side that the record gives him.

## The rules

The rules of phase two stand (`WORLD-2.md`): the world offers and never steers; Claude may write the world, never the man; one owner for every fact; every change behind a switch, and a switch set back restores the old world byte for byte.

Three are new with the fast lane:
1. **Every packet is rehearsed before it is offered to Welt.** One night of futures from one saved morning, the switch off and on.
2. **The world is scored, and he is not.** A packet goes forward only if the world's score is better with it. What he does with the world is read, never gated.
3. **His page is public.** A newspaper's text stays in his prompt and in the state folder. The day file and the page carry the headline and a link.

## Seven packets, in this order

Each is built off, tested, rehearsed on a night, and then put to Welt.

**1. The score.** No GPU. It is built on night 2's futures, so that they are the baseline.

**2. Reading returns text** (`HEGEL_PAPERS`). Welt's own point, and the base of everything that is to come back.
- **Papers he can open.** The feed already carries a summary behind each headline. `read`, where the papers are, gives him the next piece he has not read: the headline, the paper, the summary.
- **A trace the engine keeps:** the title, the date, and one line in his own words, taken from the thought that follows. No extra call.
- **Stories that run for days.** When a new headline continues one he has read, the waking line says so and gives his own line back.
- **Reading that leads somewhere.** What he has read counts as met, so that he may write and speak of it and the claim guard lets it stand. It also joins what his manuscripts and a talk may draw on. A reviewed exhibition that becomes a place comes later, with packet 7.
- **The measure:** how often something read is used later, in a thought, a letter or a talk. Today it is zero.

**3. Money as choices** (`HEGEL_EXITS`).
- **Ways out that fit him**, each a choice the record gives reasons for:
  - a second pupil through Nidhi once the lessons run;
  - a course of lectures after a talk that went well;
  - the review's column;
  - Ramesh on part time, offered before the wage falls due when the purse cannot carry it.
- **Credit with standing.** Sunil and Ramesh may let a small sum stand, once, and the record holds it. A second time depends on whether the first was paid.
- **The household notices.** A man who lives in the house sees that nothing is cooked. Shri Hegde may share a meal or speak of it. The engine decides when, and the model only what he says.
- **Two repairs of the file's act:** a refusal at Mr. Saxena's no longer wipes Mr. Malhotra's letter from the file's line, and the hearing (part 2) is drafted for Welt's pen.

**4. India answers back** (`HEGEL_REPLY`). He judged India unseen, in the lectures and in his review of 1827 on the Bhagavad Gita.
- **A scholar of Sanskrit, invented,** who has read that review and disagrees. Letters first, then meetings, then a public disputation at the IIC, which the talk scene can carry.
- **His own afterlife, findable:** a shelf at the bookseller's and in the library with what two centuries made of him. He meets it when he looks, as the 1831 gate requires.
- Claude drafts the thread with several endings, none of them chosen. Welt edits it, as he did the file's act.

**5. He starts things** (`HEGEL_UNDERTAKINGS`). The free line for wishes was hardly used. So the door is a moment, not a line.
- **Once a week, on Sunday evening, one question:** is there something you mean to begin? His answer names it, with whom, and when.
- **The engine keeps it** as an undertaking with its next date, brings it back to him on the day, and lets him drop it. Keeping time already walks him there.
- **The wish ledger** is what he asked for and the world lacked. Welt reads it weekly.

**6. People with weeks of their own, and standing** (chapter 4 of phase two, not built). Each named person gets an agenda in data that moves by date, and a record of what stands between him and them. Then the cast grows from 16 towards 40, each new person through the voices' checks.

**7. The real Delhi as supply.** Real weather and fog, real papers, the holidays and the season. The second ring of places, chosen by what the letters show he sought abroad.

4. **The page shows the headline and a link,** never a paper's text.
5. **Each packet's go,** after its night.

Evaluation rule as before: a night's futures are never training data.
