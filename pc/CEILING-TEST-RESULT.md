## By the rule declared before the run: nothing is read

The Claude family failed the calibration pairs: 4 of 8 right, against a bar of 6.
- **The four "inference" pairs:** right every time, both ways round. The family tells a sound inference from a fluent restatement.
- **The four "invention" pairs:** wrong every time on question 1. It named both pieces, or the one resting on a made-up premise, as arguing something new. **But on the same rows it flagged the made-up premise every time** (8 of 8 judgments). It saw the invention; it did not let the invention stop a claim from counting as new.

By the rule, a family that fails is shown and not used. With one family read, no verdict is declared.

## What may be said

Narrowly, and from one family that failed its calibration on one kind of pair:
- **The text alone does nothing.** A short Wikipedia introduction, handed to him, does not lift what he writes: 2 against 2.
- **The fuller instructions with time to think help a little, and halve the invented premises.** C and D win about a quarter of the briefs against A, far below the bar.
- **A stronger writer, given D's very messages, wins almost every brief.** F beats his best arm 38 to 0. All three read it so: the family as declared, the exploratory rescoring, and Welt.
- **So the limit is mostly the writer, not what he is given or how he is asked.** That is the read this test was built to make, held as likely and not as declared.

What it leaves open: whether his life in Delhi adds to what he writes (`WORLD-3.md`, the decisive experiment), and whether another local model would stand nearer F than Qwen does.
