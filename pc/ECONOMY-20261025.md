## Fixed today

- **The fallback eats out.** On Ramesh's day off, or when the kitchen is shut, a mindless step takes him to a dhaba in Khan Market for the cheapest meal (chole bhature, ₹180), if it is open and he has the cash. A world rule: no thought, no words.

- **A gap that ends at night is resumed once.** The new day used to go unsaved while he slept, so every tick made it again.

## The decision for Welt: how can a man in his position live?

1. **Leave it.** The crisis comes on 1 November with no way out: the costs accrue for ever, and he cannot even let Ramesh go. A crisis without exits is not realism; it is a trap.
2. **Exits, not gifts. Recommended.** Three world rules, each something a real Delhi would offer. Each is an offer he may refuse:
   - **Ramesh accepts less.** When the wage falls due and he cannot pay, Ramesh offers to come three days a week for ₹4,000.
   - **A cheque can be banked.** Mr. Saxena's allotment letter is proof of address; the State Bank branch in Khan Market opens an account, and a cheque clears in three days. The simpler version: the IIC pays visitors without an account in cash, against a receipt.
   - **Work that recurs.** After his first essay, the Yamuna Review asks for a fortnightly column at ₹4,000. Masterji's dealer asks about the frock again when his purse is thin.

   With these, a Hegel who works can carry about ₹13,600 a month (Ramesh part-time), while an idle one still runs into trouble.
3. **A stipend.** The Directorate pays a subsistence allowance while the file is pending. It is the simplest, and it removes the pressure and the storyline with it.

The wage falls due on 1 November, which is Day 31 on his clock, this week. Option 2 is about a day's work, with tests.
