Vienna, September 25.

My dear Marie,

The day has kept me busy in many ways, but I am well. In the morning I visited the Imperial Private Garden and the greenhouses at the Palace. One gets there through a peculiar underground passage, which the Emperor himself walks through every day. He spends some afternoon hours there. I had been looking forward to the georines and altheas, but the flowers were rather insignificant. The situation is quite different on the Peacock Island, where nature can unfold more freely.

In the afternoon I spent some hours at the Belvedere, and then in the evening I attended the performance of Rossini's *Figaro*. The performance was a true festival. Lablache sang Figaro, and Mme Fodor sang Rosine. The latter is a perfect singer; she combines beauty, grace, art, freedom, and taste in her singing in an admirable way. Lablache, a bass, is cheerful and freely comic; there is nothing low or vulgar in his performance. When the whole chorus sings and the orchestra enters with full force fortissimo, it sounds as if Lablache were singing alone. He sings this without strain, without shouting, and without a single shouting tone. Ambrogi as Dr. Bartolo was also very good, and a new singer, de Franco, also appeared. The company consists of a whole hedge of the most excellent and model singers.

The audience takes lively part in the play. Three to four performers are applauded at their first appearance. Every passage is applauded or greeted with "bravo! bravi!" After each scene the audience applauds excessively. The singer thanks the audience and leaves, but the applause continues with sustained strength, so that the singer is not merely applauded but properly "beaten" with applause. At the end of the piece there is no calling out or speaking. One cannot see the *Figaro* anywhere else in Italy with such jubilation and such an audience. I read today in a Viennese theater sheet that the most experienced people are of the opinion that, according to their longest memory, no such Italian company has been in Vienna for 50 years. It is certain that no such company will return in the next 50 years.

The party guests, whom I first had to persuade not to miss a performance, were collectively most delighted. However, they despised the music of Rossini. The music of Rossini sometimes bores me as music, but the performance was unsurpassed. The Viennese theater sheets agree in this opinion.

Kiss the children from me affectionately.

Your faithful husband and father

Hegel

My Dear,

Signora Torelli and Signora Brugnoli played the main roles; they have Roman heads, the blackest eyes and curved noses, which gave them a classical dignity.
