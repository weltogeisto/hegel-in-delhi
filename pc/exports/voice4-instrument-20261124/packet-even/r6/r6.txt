I hope this letter finds you all well and cheerful. The days here fly by quickly, and since your absence feels like the only thing missing, I am writing to you to fill the void. Yesterday I had a tiring but rewarding day. I was in the Ambras Collection and afterwards at the Belvedere. There I visited my friend Ruß, who lives nearby. He himself was not at home, but his wife and daughter showed me Dürer's engravings and other copper engravings, which I examined with great interest. Later I went for a walk with Ruß. In the evening I was at the Theater an der Wien. I had never visited this house before, and I must confess that it is the most beautiful theater building in the city. It has five rows of boxes, which are called "Stöcke" here, and no parterre boxes. Two plays with the same content were performed: A king who infiltrates a family of lowly rank in disguise. In the second play, the figure was Frederick II, who was referred to here as a Duke. Prussian names and uniforms were used. The story was based on a well-known anecdote about the King. Frederick was portrayed as humble, old, walking with a cane, and taking snuff from his breast pocket. A Prussian officer sat next to me. I had to smile, because everything said in the play about the Duke was literally observed and confirmed by the officer. The house was very empty, which was due to the enormous height of the room, which swallowed up the few spectators. Think of me when you are annoyed by the weather or disciplining the children.
